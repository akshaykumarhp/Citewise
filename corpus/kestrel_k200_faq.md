# Kestrel K-200 — Product FAQ

*Customer-facing · Product line: Kestrel inspection drones*

## What is the Kestrel K-200?

The Kestrel K-200 is Halcyon Robotics' autonomous inspection drone for wind turbines, bridges and power lines. It launched in June 2024 and replaced the Kestrel K-150.

## How long does the battery last?

Flight time is up to 42 minutes with no payload and 31 minutes with the full 1.2 kg sensor payload. A full recharge of the KB-90 battery pack takes 70 minutes.

## What is the maximum wind speed it can fly in?

The K-200 is rated for sustained winds of up to 12 m/s and gusts of up to 15 m/s.

## Which firmware version is current?

The current stable firmware is v3.8.1, released in August 2025. Version v3.8.1 fixes the compass calibration drift reported in v3.8.0. Firmware updates are delivered over the air through the Kestrel Hub app.

## What sensors are included?

The standard payload includes a 48 MP visual camera and a 640x512 thermal camera. A LiDAR module (KL-3) is available as an optional add-on.

## How far can it fly from the controller?

The maximum control range is 8 km in unobstructed line of sight. Operators are responsible for complying with local regulations, which often restrict flights to visual line of sight.

## What is the operating temperature range?

The K-200 operates between -10 °C and 45 °C.

## What warranty does it come with?

The K-200 comes with a 2-year limited hardware warranty. Batteries are covered for 12 months or 300 charge cycles, whichever comes first. The optional Kestrel Care plan extends hardware coverage to 4 years and includes one accidental damage replacement.

## Where is flight data stored?

Flight telemetry is uploaded to the Kestrel cloud platform, hosted in the EU (Frankfurt region). Customers can export their data at any time in CSV or Parquet format.
